# About

I'm a network and infrastructure engineer who likes turning complicated systems
into things that are easier to operate.

Most of my work sits somewhere between networking and software. I've worked on
large-scale infrastructure at companies including Cloudflare and DigitalOcean,
building systems around network automation, distributed systems, observability,
and infrastructure management. A recurring theme has been taking something that
works manually or doesn't scale particularly well, and turning it into a system
that can be operated reliably and predictably.

I got to help build and evolve the networks at both Cloudflare and DigitalOcean,
at times when reliability and customer trust mattered just as much as adding
capacity and new features. Both companies eventually went public. Coincidence?
I don't think so! More seriously, building infrastructure that customers can
trust is a pretty direct part of enabling a company to grow, and it has been one
of the most rewarding parts of the work for me.

More recently, I've been working in the financial domain, where the networks
and infrastructure tend to come with a slightly different set of constraints.
Low latency, reliability, visibility, and understanding exactly what is
happening become particularly important, and I've been spending more time
exploring how networking, automation, and software can fit together in that
environment.

I've also been involved in network automation and open source before it was
cool. I contributed to projects such as
[NAPALM](https://github.com/napalm-automation/napalm) and Salt, and was
involved in building some of the tooling and community around them. I was
awarded SaltStack Contributor of the Year in 2017. I'm also the author of
[Network Automation at Scale](https://www.oreilly.com/library/view/network-automation-at/9781491992524/)
from O'Reilly. See [Projects](/projects/) for more (public) projects I've
worked on.

I'm comfortable working across quite a few languages and tend to use whatever
is appropriate for the problem rather than having a particular favourite. Over
the years that has included Python, Go, C/C++, Java, Ruby, and quite a bit of
JavaScript. The language is usually the easy part; understanding the system
you're trying to build is the interesting bit.

These days I'm particularly interested in multi-cloud networking, network
security, observability, and building systems that can reason about and operate
infrastructure as a whole rather than treating every component in isolation.

This site is where I write about some of that, even though I don't always have
the time. Usually the things I've built, broken, learned, or found interesting
along the way.
